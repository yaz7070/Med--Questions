Thrombocytopenia

Platelets under 150 × 10⁹/L. First prove it is real, grade it, then ask why: the marrow isn't making them, the spleen is holding them, or something is destroying them.

Suspected DVT or PE

Score the pretest probability first. A low score plus a negative D-dimer rules out clot. A positive D-dimer proves nothing, so it only sends you to imaging. Virchow's triad: stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability.

Anticoagulants

Sort every drug by one question: does it need antithrombin to work? Then remember three things for each: how you monitor it, how it is cleared, and how you reverse it.

Ask

Indirect: needs antithrombin

Heparins boost antithrombin

    • Inhibits IIa = Xa · IV/SC in hospital

      Unfractionated heparin

      Monitor

      Drugs

      Stop

      Monitor
      Usually none; anti-Xa

      17–21 h
      Cleared

Vitamin K antagonist

Stops the liver activating factors

Warfarin

How
Blocks VKORC1, so factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S aren't activated. The effect mostly reflects low thrombin
Route
Oral
Monitor
PT/INR every 1–2 days at first, then every 4–6 weeks. Target 2.0–3.0
Half-life
36–42 h
Stop
5 days before major procedure
Reverse
Vitamin K + prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC)
Use
Mechanical heart valves, valve disease, arterial clots, AF, VTE in renal failure
Avoid
Pregnancy, unreliable patients with no INR monitoring, liver disease

Many drug and food interactions. Proteins C and S fall first, so the patient is briefly hypercoagulable when warfarin starts; overlap with heparin.
