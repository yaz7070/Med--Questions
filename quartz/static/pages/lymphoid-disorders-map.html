Lymphoma

Lymphoma is a cancer of lymphocytes that builds up in lymph nodes and lymphoid tissue. It causes lymphadenopathy, extranodal disease and constitutional symptoms.

Node biopsy with immunohistochemistry and flow cytometryB cells: CD19, CD20, CD79a. T cells: CD3, CD5, CD7
Are there Reed-Sternberg cells?

Yes: Hodgkin lymphoma (15%)

Reed-Sternberg cells in an inflammatory background. Younger patients, men more than women. Nodal, spreads to the next node group in order. No monoclonal pattern on flow.

  • Stain the malignant cells
    • CD15 and CD30 positive, CD20 and CD45 negative: classical (95%)

      Nodular sclerosis

      The most common Hodgkin lymphoma (70%), seen in younger adults.

      Clues
      Mediastinal mass, lacunar cells, nodules with collagen bands
      Outlook
      Mostly stage I to II. Very good to excellent

      Mixed cellularity

      Classical Hodgkin of older adults (20%), tied to HIV and EBV.

      Clues
      Most frequent type in HIV, EBV in 75%, peripheral nodes
      Outlook
      Mostly higher stage with B symptoms. Worse than nodular sclerosis

      Lymphocyte rich

      A rare type (under 5%) with very few Reed-Sternberg cells among many benign lymphocytes.

      Outlook
      Excellent

      Lymphocyte depleted

      The rarest type (1%): numerous bizarre Reed-Sternberg cells and almost no benign lymphocytes.

      Outlook
      Always high stage with B symptoms. Worst prognosis
    • CD20 and CD45 positive, CD15 and CD30 negative

      Nodular lymphocyte predominant

      A separate, slow Hodgkin lymphoma (5%) built from "popcorn" LP cells.

      Clues
      No EBV link, mediastinal mass rare, no light chain restriction
      Outlook
      Mostly early stage, no B symptoms. Excellent, but recurs often
Treat every patient, aiming for cureStage I to II: short course of chemotherapy, then involved-field radiation. Stage III to IV or bulky disease: ABVD × 2 then AVD × 4, plus radiation if a PET-positive mass remains

No: non-Hodgkin lymphoma (85%)

Malignant B, T or NK cells, with over 50 subtypes. Older patients. Nodal or extranodal, and spread can skip regions. Monoclonal pattern on flow.

  • How does it behave, and what does the biopsy show?
    • Painless, rapidly enlarging mass with B symptoms

      Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)

      The most common NHL (50%): aggressive and rapidly fatal untreated, but it responds well to treatment.

      Biopsy
      Normal node structure lost, large nuclei with visible nucleoli, high mitotic activity
      Treat
      CHOP-R. 3 cycles for stage I to II, 6 cycles for stage III to IV
      Outlook
      Scored with the IPI. Germinal centre type does better than activated B-cell type
    • Slow, often no symptoms, follicular pattern

      Follicular lymphoma

      The second most common NHL (30 to 40%): an indolent B-cell lymphoma that relapses and remits and is mostly incurable.

      Gene
      t(14;18), Bcl-2 activation
      Stage
      Usually III or IV at diagnosis
      Treat
      Watch and wait until symptoms develop. Stage I: radiation, potentially curable. Stage II to IV: 6 cycles of BR, then 2 years of rituximab
      Outlook
      Scored with the FLIPI
    • High lymphocyte count, smudge cells on the film

      CLL / small lymphocytic lymphoma

      An indolent, incurable B-cell cancer of older adults that sits in nodes, marrow, spleen and usually the blood.

      Clues
      Lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly. About 15% have autoantibodies against red cells
      Treat
      Do not treat low-risk or asymptomatic disease. Later: chlorambucil, fludarabine, anti-CD20 antibody, ibrutinib
      Outlook
      Median survival 10 to 15 years if caught early
    • A signature translocation

      Burkitt lymphoma

      B-cell lymphoma with a starry-sky pattern. t(8;14), c-Myc activation.

      Mantle cell lymphoma

      B-cell lymphoma that overexpresses cyclin D1. t(11;14).

      MALT lymphoma

      Lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue. t(11;18), MALT1 activation.

      Anaplastic large cell lymphoma

      T-cell lymphoma with an ALK1 mutation. t(2;5).

Plasma cell disorders

Plasma cells are mature B cells that make antibodies. In these disorders one clone makes a single non-functional antibody, the M protein.

Suspect itFatigue, infections, bone pain (spine most often), hypercalcemia, impaired kidney function
Find the M proteinProtein electrophoresis of blood and 24-hour urine, serum free light chains. Blood film shows rouleaux
Bone marrow plasma cells and CRAB organ damage

Mediastinal mass

The mediastinum is the space between the lungs. The compartment the mass sits in, and how fast it appeared, give you the short list.

Chest x-ray shows a widened mediastinumOver 6 cm on an upright PA film, or over 8 cm on a supine AP film
