Chart 1

A count is high

For every high count, ask one question: is this the marrow reacting to something else (secondary, the common answer), or is the marrow itself abnormal (primary, often a cancer)? Secondary causes are far more common, so rule them out first.

Order
CBC with differential and peripheral blood filmWhich line is high? If Hb, platelets and WBC are all high, treat it as erythrocytosis and think of PV

Erythrocytosis

Too many red cells

Men Hb > 170 g/L · women Hb > 160 g/L
  • Order
    Serum EPO levelTells you whether the drive comes from EPO or from the marrow
    • EPO low: primary

      Polycythemia vera

      Why
      JAK2 mutation makes the marrow grow without EPO, so the kidney turns EPO down
      Confirm
      JAK2 V617F or exon 12
      Rare
      Congenital EPO receptor (EPOR) mutation

      Go to MPNs ↓

    • EPO normal or high: secondary

      Secondary erythrocytosis

      Systemic hypoxia
      COPD, right-to-left shunt, smoking, OSA, high altitude
      Kidney hypoxia
      Renal artery stenosis, renal cysts, after renal transplant
      EPO tumours
      Cerebellar hemangioblastoma, RCC, HCC, pheochromocytoma
      Drugs
      EPO doping, androgens (testosterone)
      Congenital
      Oxygen-sensing defects, high-affinity Hb (left-shifted curve)
      Treat
      The underlying cause

Also seen after splenectomy or with hyposplenism, because old cells aren't cleared.

Other high white cells (benign causes)

CellThink
Eosinophils (> 0.5)Drugs (penicillin), asthma, eczema, parasites, Churg-Strauss, Loeffler pneumonia, vasculitis

Workup for unexplained leukocytosis: infection screen (EBV, CMV, hepatitis, HIV, TB, toxoplasmosis), CXR, abdominal ultrasound, then cytogenetics and molecular tests (BCR-ABL, JAK2).

Chart 2

A count is low

Low counts have three causes: the marrow isn't making cells, cells are destroyed, or the spleen holds them. Low neutrophils are the dangerous one. A fever with severe neutropenia is an emergency.

Ask
Is one line low, or all three?

Pancytopenia

All three lines low

Anemia (fatigue) + low platelets (mucocutaneous bleeding) + low WBC (recurrent infections)
  • Order
    Film, retic count, B12 and folate, spleen exam or ultrasoundThen bone marrow biopsy if the cause isn't clear
    • Marrow empty, infiltrated or dysplastic

      Decreased production

      Causes
      Aplastic anemia and congenital marrow failure, MDS, CMML, AML, myelofibrosis, myelophthisis (marrow infiltration), B12/folate deficiency
      Clue
      Low retic count. Teardrops and leukoerythroblastic film with fibrosis or infiltration
    • Marrow active, cells destroyed

      Increased destruction

      Causes
      Immune, medications, infections, connective tissue disease, idiopathic
    • Big spleen

      Sequestration

      Cause
      Splenomegaly holds all three cell types

Neutropenia

Low neutrophils

ANC < 1.5 · mild 1–1.5 · moderate 0.5–0.99 · severe < 0.5 × 10⁹/L
  • Ask first
    Does the patient have a fever?Single oral temp > 38.3 °C, or ≥ 38.0 °C for over 1 hour
    • Fever + ANC < 0.5

      Febrile neutropenia

      Next
      Oncologic emergency: antibiotics within the hour

      Go to emergencies ↓

    • Stable: find the cause
      Ask
      Lifelong or new?Drug list, recent infections, autoimmune symptoms, diet, other cytopenias
      • New: acquired (common)

        Acquired neutropenia

        Infection
        Viral (EBV, CMV, hepatitis, HIV, influenza), sepsis, TB, Brucella, Rickettsia
        Drugs
        Chemotherapy, radiation, antibiotics, antithyroid drugs, clozapine, antiepileptics
        Autoimmune
        Primary autoimmune, SLE, RA (Felty syndrome)
        Nutrition
        B12, folate, copper deficiency, malnutrition
        Marrow
        MDS, AML, lymphoma or metastatic tumour in the marrow
        Spleen
        Splenic sequestration
      • Since childhood: congenital

        Congenital neutropenia

        Types
        Constitutional (benign), cyclic neutropenia, severe congenital (Kostmann), Chediak-Higashi, Shwachman-Diamond, Barth syndrome
Chart 3

Myeloproliferative neoplasms

A clonal marrow stem cell overproduces one or more myeloid lines. The cell line that is most out of control names the disease: PV is a red cell cancer, ET is a platelet cancer, PMF scars the marrow. Rare; mean age over 60, slightly more common in men.

Suspect
High counts with no secondary causeSplenomegaly, erythromelalgia (burning red hands and feet), clots, ocular migraines
Order
JAK2 V617F (± exon 12), CALR, MPL, and BCR-ABLBCR-ABL positive means CML, not one of these three

Hb and hematocrit high

JAK2 in 90–95%

Polycythemia vera

Clues
Aquagenic pruritus (itch after a hot bath), ruddy face, erythromelalgia, gout, splenomegaly, headache, dizziness
Major
1. Hb > 165 (men) or > 160 (women), or Hct > 49% / 48%
2. Hypercellular marrow, all three lines (panmyelosis)
3. JAK2 V617F or exon 12
Minor
Low serum EPO
Diagnose
All 3 major, or first 2 major + minor
Treat
ASA 81 mg daily and keep Hct < 0.45. Low risk: phlebotomy. High risk (≥ 60 or prior clot): hydroxyurea ± phlebotomy
Prognosis
Untreated 2–3 years; treated slightly reduced

Platelets high

JAK2 50–60%, CALR or MPL in many of the rest

Essential thrombocythemia

Clues
Erythromelalgia, ocular migraines, mild bleeding, splenomegaly is rare
Major
1. Platelets ≥ 450
2. Marrow: mainly large, mature megakaryocytes
3. Doesn't meet criteria for CML, PV, PMF, MDS
4. JAK2, CALR or MPL mutation
Minor
Clonal marker, or no reactive cause
Diagnose
All 4 major, or first 3 major + minor
Exclude
Infection, inflammation, cancer, iron deficiency
Treat
ASA; hydroxyurea if high risk; control CV risk factors
Prognosis
Treated: close to normal lifespan

Scarred marrow, cytopenias

JAK2 50–60%, CALR or MPL

Primary myelofibrosis

Clues
Fatigue, early satiety and weight loss (huge spleen), bruising, B symptoms
Film
Teardrop cells, leukoerythroblastic picture, pancytopenia
Major
1. Atypical megakaryocytes with reticulin or collagen fibrosis (grade 2–3)
2. JAK2, CALR or MPL (or other clonal marker)
3. Not another myeloid neoplasm
Minor
Anemia, WBC ≥ 11, palpable spleen, ↑ LDH, leukoerythroblastosis
Diagnose
All 3 major + ≥ 1 minor (confirmed twice)
Treat
Individual goals. Cure: allogeneic stem cell transplant. Symptom control: transfusion, steroids, hydroxyurea, EPO, ruxolitinib (JAK inhibitor)
Note
PV and ET can progress into myelofibrosis

Chart 4

Leukemias

Leukemia is a white cell cancer in the blood and marrow. Two questions name it: are the cells immature blasts (acute) or mature-looking (chronic)? Are they myeloid or lymphoid? Most common cancer in young patients.

Suspect
Marrow failure signsFatigue (anemia), bleeding (low platelets), infections and fever (low neutrophils), bone pain, hepatosplenomegaly, gum or skin infiltration (leukemia cutis)
Look
Blood film: blasts?Then marrow aspirate and biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics (karyotype, FISH), molecular tests

Acute: blasts

Precursors stop maturing and crowd out the marrow. Rapid onset

  • Order
    Flow cytometry and morphologyMyeloid or lymphoid blasts?
    • Myeloid: large blasts, Auer rods

      AML

      Who
      80% of adult acute leukemia, 15% of childhood
      Labs
      ↑ LDH, uric acid, phosphate; ↓ Ca, ↓ K. Check INR, aPTT, fibrinogen for DIC
      Before chemo
      CXR (pneumonia), ECG, MUGA scan (heart function)
      Treat
      Induction chemo (7+3), then consolidation chemo or allogeneic stem cell transplant (high risk). Supportive transfusions and antibiotics. 4–6 weeks in hospital
      Prognosis
      Children 70% remission. Much worse over 55
      FAB
      M3 = promyelocytic (APL). M4/M5 = monocytic
      • Many Auer rods, t(15;17), HLA-DR negative

        Acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL)

        Who
        10–15% of AML, median age 40
        Confirm
        t(15;17) on karyotype; PML-RARα fusion on FISH or NGS
        Types
        Hypergranular (M3, 75%, WBC low). Microgranular (M3v, 25%, WBC > 10)
        Risks
        DIC and fibrinolysis, fatal brain bleeds, TLS, leukostasis, febrile neutropenia
        Treat
        All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) + arsenic; chemo if high risk
        Emergency: high early death from bleeding. Start ATRA as soon as APL is suspected
    • Lymphoid: blasts with no granules

      ALL

      Who
      80% of childhood leukemia, 15–20% of adult
      Look for
      Mediastinal mass on CXR; CNS spread (LP before chemo); testes
      Treat
      2–3 years (Dana-Farber): induction, consolidation or transplant, maintenance, CNS prophylaxis (intrathecal chemo or radiation). CAR T-cell, blinatumomab, inotuzumab
      Prognosis
      Children about 90% cured. Adults: 75% remission, 40% long-term survival

Chronic: mature cells

Cells still mature but overproduce. Slow onset, often found on a routine CBC

  • Look
    Which line is expanded?
    • Granulocytes at every stage, ↑ basophils

      CML

      Why
      Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) → BCR-ABL fusion, a constantly active tyrosine kinase
      Film
      Bimodal myelocytes and neutrophils, ↑ basophils and eosinophils, ↑ platelets
      Phases
      1. Chronic (often no symptoms) → 2. Accelerated → 3. Blast crisis (acts like acute leukemia)
      Clues
      Fatigue, weight loss, sweats, big spleen, itch
      Confirm
      FISH or karyotype for t(9;22); Q-RT-PCR for BCR-ABL (also tracks response)
      Treat
      Tyrosine kinase inhibitors: imatinib (Gleevec), nilotinib, dasatinib. Allopurinol to prevent TLS. Stem cell transplant possibly curative
    • Mature B lymphocytes, smudge cells

      CLL

      Who
      Most common leukemia overall; older adults, median 70, M:F 2:1
      Clues
      Often no symptoms. Lymphadenopathy, big liver and spleen, B symptoms
      Autoimmune
      Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, ITP, low immunoglobulins (infections)
      Confirm
      Flow cytometry: CD5, CD19, CD23, dim CD20, one light chain (clonal)
      Stage
      Rai: 0 low, 1–2 intermediate, 3–4 high risk
      Treat
      Incurable. Do not treat early or asymptomatic disease. When needed: chlorambucil, fludarabine, anti-CD20 antibodies, ibrutinib (BTK inhibitor)
      Prognosis
      Median survival 10–15 years if found early

      Related: small lymphocytic lymphoma (same cells, mainly in nodes) and monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis (small clone, no disease).

How the marrow is tested

MorphologyAspirate shows every stage of maturation. Normal myeloid:erythroid ratio 1:1 to 3:1. Core biopsy shows cellularity (normal ≈ 100 − age %)
SitePosterior superior iliac spine. Sternum for aspirate only (never a core: too close to the heart)
Flow cytometryLight scatter (size, granularity) + labelled antibodies give the immunophenotype: immature, myeloid, B, T or NK markers
CytogeneticsChromosome changes: karyotype, FISH
MolecularGene mutations: PCR, next-generation sequencing
Chart 5

Hematologic emergencies

These complications kill patients early, often before or just after treatment starts. Match the presentation to the emergency, then act.

Ask
Leukemia patient or on chemo, and suddenly unwell: what's the main problem?

Fever + neutropenia

ANC < 0.5 and temp > 38.3 once, or ≥ 38.0 for 1 h

Febrile neutropenia

  1. Stabilize: ABCs, IV fluids, oxygen if hypoxic
  2. Investigate: CBC + differential, electrolytes, creatinine, LFTs, bilirubin, blood and urine cultures, CXR
  3. Examine: mouth and sinuses, lungs, abdomen (no DRE), skin and IV line sites, mental status
  4. Treat empirically: piperacillin-tazobactam or a carbapenem (meropenem, imipenem)
  5. Add vancomycin if skin or line infection, or hemodynamically unstable
Bacteria
Gram negative (E. coli, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella); gram positive (S. aureus, strep, enterococci)
Viral
HSV, CMV, VZV reactivation
Fungal
Candida, Aspergillus, mucor
Don't wait for cultures. Antibiotics within the hour

Electrolytes off after chemo

Or spontaneous with fast-growing tumours

Tumour lysis syndrome

Why
Dying tumour cells release their contents into the blood
Labs
↑ uric acid, ↑ potassium, ↑ phosphate, ↑ creatinine, ↓ calcium (phosphate binds it), acidosis
Effects
Acute kidney injury, arrhythmias, seizures, death
Prevent
Hydration and allopurinol before chemo
Treat
Aggressive IV hydration, rasburicase (breaks down uric acid), allopurinol (stops new uric acid), frequent labs, dialysis

Very high blasts + organ symptoms

AML or ALL

Leukostasis

Why
Big, sticky blasts plug small vessels → local hypoxia → cytokines → endothelial damage
Lungs
Dyspnea, hypoxia
Brain
Confusion, headache, visual changes
Other
Fever, cardiac ischemia, AKI, priapism, limb or bowel ischemia
Test
CXR, ECG, cardiac enzymes
Treat
IV fluids, leukapheresis, cytoreduction (lower the blast count fast)
