Iron deficiency anemia

Iron runs out in order: stores empty first, then plasma iron falls, then the marrow stops making full cells. Every lab follows that sequence.

Ferritin↓Ferritin is the storage shell for iron. Stores are spent first, so ferritin drops before anything else. Most specific test for IDA.
Serum iron↓With stores empty, nothing refills the small plasma pool (about 4 mg) that carries iron to the marrow.
TIBC / transferrin↑The liver senses iron need and makes more transferrin to catch every free iron atom. More empty transferrin means more binding capacity.
Transferrin sat.↓Saturation is serum iron ÷ TIBC. A low top over a high bottom gives the lowest saturation of any TAILS cause.
RDW↑Cells made after iron ran out are smaller than older cells made before. Two sizes in one sample widen the spread (anisocytosis).
RBC count↓Without iron the marrow makes fewer cells, not only smaller ones. Mentzer index (MCV ÷ RBC) sits above 13.
sTfR, zinc protoporphyrin↑Starved precursors put out more transferrin receptors. Protoporphyrin has no iron to receive, so zinc slots in instead.
Film
Hypochromic, anisocytosis, poikilocytosis, elliptocytes (pencil cells). Target cells in severe cases.
Clues
Pica, koilonychia, glossitis, restless legs, esophageal webs. Heavy menses, GI bleed, celiac, vegan diet, pregnancy.
Next
Find the source. An older adult with no obvious loss needs a GI workup for colon cancer. Then oral or IV iron, transfusion if severe.

Anemia of chronic inflammation

Cytokines (IL-6) drive the liver to release hepcidin. Hepcidin blocks ferroportin, the iron exit channel on gut cells and macrophages. Iron sits in stores while the marrow goes without (functional iron deficiency).

FerritinN/↑Iron is trapped inside macrophages, so stores read full. Ferritin is also an acute-phase reactant and rises with inflammation itself.
Serum iron↓Ferroportin is blocked, so iron never leaves stores or gut cells for the plasma. This withholds iron from microbes.
TIBC / transferrin↓Transferrin is a negative acute-phase protein, so the liver makes less. Full-looking stores give no signal to raise it.
Transferrin sat.↓/NIron and TIBC both fall, so the ratio drops less than in IDA.
RDWNProduction is slow but even, so cell size stays uniform.
sTfRNPrecursors are less starved than in IDA. A normal sTfR separates ACI from IDA hidden by a high ferritin.
Film
Hypochromic. Starts normocytic and turns microcytic over time.
Clues
TB, osteomyelitis, endocarditis, lung abscess, HIV. RA, SLE, IBD. Lymphoma, carcinoma, myeloma. Alcoholic liver or heart disease.
Next
Treat the underlying disease. EPO in severe cases. Seldom needs transfusion.

Sideroblastic anemia or lead

Iron reaches the precursor and enters the mitochondria, but heme synthesis is broken. Iron never joins protoporphyrin and piles up in mitochondria ringed around the nucleus. B6 is the cofactor for ALA synthase. Lead blocks ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase.

Ferritin↑Unused iron builds up in stores. Ineffective erythropoiesis also raises gut iron absorption, pushing toward overload.
Serum iron↑The marrow fails to use the iron it receives, so plasma iron stays high.
TIBC / transferrinN/↓The body is iron replete or overloaded, so the liver has no drive to make more transferrin.
Transferrin sat.↑High iron over a normal or low TIBC.
RDW↑Dimorphic blood: normal cells from unaffected precursors mixed with small hypochromic ones.
Film
Basophilic stippling (RNA precipitates, lead blocks the enzyme that clears RNA). Pappenheimer bodies (iron granules).
Confirm
Bone marrow with iron stain: ringed sideroblasts. Blood lead level if exposure.
Clues
Alcohol, B6 deficiency, anti-TB drugs, alkylator chemo, MDS, X-linked family history, lead exposure.
Next
Remove the toxin, chelation for lead, pyridoxine in some cases, transfusion. Marrow transplant rarely.

Thalassemia (suspect)

A quantitative α or β globin gene defect. Iron is fine. The cell runs short of globin chains to wrap around heme.

Ferritin, Fe, TIBC, sat.NThe defect sits in globin, so iron supply, transport and stores are untouched. In major forms, transfusion and extra absorption push iron up.
RBC count↑The marrow is healthy and EPO drives it hard, so it turns out many small cells. Hb falls only mildly despite a low MCV.
Mentzer index< 13Low MCV over a high RBC count gives a small ratio. IDA sits above 13 because both values fall.
RDWNIn trait, every cell is small from the start, so sizes stay uniform.
Film
Target cells (excess membrane for the Hb inside), basophilic stippling. Teardrops and hemolysis in severe forms.
Next
Hb electrophoresis or HPLC to split α from β.

β-thalassemia

Fewer β chains leave α chains without partners. Free α chains precipitate, kill precursors in the marrow and shorten red cell life.

HbA2 (α2δ2)↑The cell makes more δ chains to pair with spare α. Elevated HbA2 defines β-thal minor (heterozygous).
HbF (α2γ2)↑γ chains fill in for missing β. Marked in β-thal major (homozygous), which shows from 3–6 months as HbF switch-off fails.
Spectrum
Minor: mild anemia. Intermedia: bone disease, splenomegaly. Major: transfusion-dependent from childhood, bone deformities.
Next
Transfuse by severity, chelate iron (deferoxamine), genetic counselling.

Southeast Asian or African ancestry

α-thalassemia

HbA, HbA2 and HbF all need α chains, so a short α supply has no backup chain to raise. That is why electrophoresis reads normal in trait.

HbA2NNo compensatory shift is possible, so the ratio of A to A2 stays normal.
HbH (β4) / Bart's (γ4)↑Spare β chains pair with each other in adults (HbH). Spare γ chains do the same in the fetus (Bart's). Both hold O₂ poorly, and HbH precipitates to cause hemolysis.
Spectrum
1 gene: silent carrier. 2: trait, mild anemia. 3: HbH disease, hemolysis, splenomegaly. 4: Hb Bart's, hydrops fetalis.
Confirm
Gene sequencing, HbH inclusion body test.

What each test measures

  • Hemoglobin: iron in circulating cells, about 2500 mg. Overall measure of iron metabolism.
  • Serum iron / transferrin: plasma iron, about 4 mg. Delivery to the marrow.
  • Ferritin: body stores, about 1500 mg. Only a trace leaks into serum.
  • TIBC: how much more iron transferrin will hold. Moves opposite to the body's iron need.

Traps

  • Ferritin rises with inflammation. A sick patient with ferritin in the normal range still has possible IDA, often mixed with ACI.
  • ACI starts normocytic. A microcytic picture means it has run a long course.
  • Normal iron studies plus normal electrophoresis in a microcytic patient points to α-thal trait, not a normal workup.
  • Basophilic stippling appears in lead, sideroblastic anemia and thalassemia. Use iron studies and ancestry to split them.
