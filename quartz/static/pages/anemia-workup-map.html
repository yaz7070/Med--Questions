Microcytic

Not enough hemoglobin is made, so the precursor keeps dividing and the cell ends up small and pale. Either heme or globin is short.

TAILS: thalassemia · anemia of chronic inflammation · iron deficiency · lead · sideroblastic

    Serum ferritinBest single marker of iron stores
    • Ferritin low

      Iron deficiency anemia

      Labs

      Film

      Clues
      Pica, koilonychia, glossitis, heavy menses, GI bleeding, celiac, vegan diet, pregnancy
      Next

      Order

        Why

        Clues

        Next

        Why

        Film

        Confirm

        Remove the toxin, pyridoxine, chelation for lead

          Spectrum
          Minor: ↑ HbA2, mild. Major: ↑ HbF, transfusion-dependent from childhood
          Film

          Next

MCV 80–100 fL

Normocytic

Cells are normal-sized. Ask whether the marrow is failing to make enough, or making plenty and losing them.

Macrocytic

Either DNA synthesis fails so the cell grows without dividing, or the membrane carries extra lipid.

ABCDEF: alcohol/liver · B12 · compensatory retics · drugs/dysplasia · endocrine · folate/fetus

Hemolytic anemia

Red cells are destroyed early. The marrow is healthy and compensates, so the retic count is high. Heme becomes bilirubin and haptoglobin is used up mopping free Hb.

Confirm hemolysis
↑ reticulocytes↑ LDH↑ indirect bilirubin↓ haptoglobin

Intravascular hemolysis adds hemoglobinuria and hemosiderinuria (dark urine). Extravascular hemolysis happens in the spleen and liver and causes splenomegaly.

      Hereditary spherocytosis

      Why
      Autosomal dominant defect in spectrin, ankyrin, band 3 or protein 4.2
      Clues
      Family history, splenomegaly, jaundice, pigment gallstones
      Confirm
      Flow cytometry for band 3 (EMA binding)
      Next
      Folate, vaccines, penicillin prophylaxis, splenectomy if severe
    • Bite cells, Heinz bodies

      G6PD deficiency

      Why
      X-linked. No glutathione defence, so oxidants denature Hb
      Clues
      Episode after fava beans, infection, sulfonamides, antimalarials, ASA
      Next
      Avoid triggers. Enzyme assay can read falsely normal during a crisis
    • Schistocytes

      Microangiopathic or mechanical

      Causes
      TTP, HUS, DIC, malignant hypertension, pre-eclampsia; mechanical heart valves
      Next
      Platelets, coagulation tests, creatinine. Suspected TTP is an emergency
    • Sickle cells

      Sickle cell disease

      Why
      Autosomal recessive. HbS: glutamate → valine in the β chain
      Clues
      Vaso-occlusive crises: dactylitis, acute chest syndrome, stroke
      Confirm
      Hb electrophoresis or HPLC; newborn screening
      Next
      Hydroxyurea, folate, penicillin to age 5, vaccines
