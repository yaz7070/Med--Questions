## The Coulter Counter

- Coulter counter: an instrument used in laboratories to enumerate blood cells and measure their indices

Setup: a tube with a small aperture on the wall is
immersed into a beaker that contains particles suspended
in a low-concentration electrolyte

Aperture: as a particle passes through the aperture, a
volume of electrolyte equivalent to the immersed volume
of the particle is displaced from the sensing zone

Electrodes: two electrodes, one inside the aperture tube
and one outside the tube but inside the beaker, are
placed and a current path is provided by the electrolyte
when an electric field is applied

## Hemoglobin

- Transporter protein that makes up 90% of RBC mass
- Tetramer: adult hemoglobin (HbA) = 2 𝛂 + 2 𝛃 globin chains; fetal hemoglobin (HbF) = 2 𝛂 + 2 𝜸 globin chains
  - 4 𝛂 globins are made by 2 genes on chromosome 16
  - 2 𝛃 globins are made by 1 gene on chromosome 11
- Each globin chain is attached to a heme complex (Fe2+ ion + porphyrin); each Fe2+ ion can bind one oxygen molecule
- Each hemoglobin molecule can bind up to 4 oxygen molecules
- Fetal hemoglobin, HbF (α2γ2), switches to adult forms HbA (α2β2) and HbA2 (α2δ2) at 3-6 months of life

## Myelodysplastic Syndromes

- Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS): form a group of clonal hematopoietic stem cell malignancies characterized by ineffective
hematopoiesis in one or more cell lineages, associated peripheral cytopenias, and risk of transformation to AML
- 3-4 per 100,000; much more common in elderly patients (1 in 1000); median age = 65-70 years
- Etiology: idiopathic or secondary to toxic exposures (chemotherapy, radiation, environmental toxins), may be associated with an
underlying hereditary disorder or acquired mutations
- Clinical presentation: anemia (80% at diagnosis, usually normocytic or macrocytic); neutropenia (40% at diagnosis),
thrombocytopenia (30% at diagnosis), progression in AML
- Ix: CBC (penias), ferritin (iron overload), BM aspirate/biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics
- International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS): score is based on the number of blasts in the BM, chromosomal abnormalities,
and number of cell lines involved
- Lower-risk management: improve QoL, transfusions (&lt; 70 gm/L), manage iron overload, growth factor support (EPO, G-CSF)
- Higher-risk management: alter course of disease/slow progression; transfusions (&lt; 70 gm/L), hypomethylating agents
(azacytidine, decitabine), high dose chemotherapy, hematopoietic stem cell transplant
- MDS with del(5q) occurs in &lt; 10% of all MDS à macrocytic anemia, thrombocytosis; treat with lenalidomide (Revlimid)

## Iron Metabolism

- 20 mg of iron is recycled every day to make new RBCs – absorb and lose 1-2 mg/day
- Molecular iron is not soluble and free ionic iron is toxic (bound to transferrin when transported in the blood)
- Iron is the centerpiece in the heme molecule; malfunctioning of enzymes in heme biosynthesis can lead to porphyria
- Ferritin: an iron storage protein composed of 24 identical protein subunits that form a hollow shell; can pack 4500 iron ions
- Heme iron is bound to heme and taken up by heme carrier proteins (HCP); transported out by ferroportin and hephaestin
  - Hepcidin can block ferroportin with iron concentrations are too high
- Non-heme iron (Fe3+) is converted to Fe2+ by duodenal cytochrome B with the help of ascorbic acid, and then taken up by
divalent metal transporter

| Blood Test | Measures |
|---|---|
| Hemoglobin | Hemoglobin available in circulating erythrocytes (2500 mg) à overall measure of iron metabolism |
| Serum iron/transferrin | Plasma content (4 mg) à iron delivery to bone marrow |
| Serum ferritin | Body iron stores (1500 mg) à small amount present in serum; chelation if > 1000 μg/L |

## Folate (Vitamin B9) Metabolism

- 400 μg/day required; total body stores in the liver (10-12 mg) are sufficient for 3-4 months
- Complete lack of dietary folate takes months before deficiency develops as healthy adults have 500-20,000 µg of folate stored
- Folate is irreversibly converted to tetrahydrofolic acid (THF), which acts as a cofactor in DNA metabolism (uracil à thymine)
- 5-methyl-THF is irreversibly converted to THF by methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR)
  - Methionine synthase: homocysteine à methionine (methylation)

## Cobalamin (Vitamin B12) Metabolism

- 1-2 mcg/day required; total body stores in the liver (2-5 mg) are sufficient for 3-5 years
- Free vitamin B12 binds to intrinsic factor (IF) in the duodenum, and IF-B12 complex is absorbed in the ileum
- Vitamin B12 is a cofactor in two reactions:
  - Methionine synthase: homocysteine à methionine (methylation)
  - Methylmalonyl CoA mutase: methylmalonyl CoA à succinyl CoA (TCA cycle)
- Pernicious anemia: caused by impaired uptake of vitamin B12 due to a lack of IF in the gastric mucosa
