## JAK2 Pathophysiology

- Wild-type JAK2 is present on RBC precursor cells in the BM
  - Requires binding of EPO to change the receptor conformation to phosphorylate the JAK kinase for activation of the
downstream STAT, PI3K, and RAS-MAPK pathways for proliferation
- JAK2 mutation is an acquired somatic mutation located in the negative regulatory pseudokinase JH2 domain on chromosome 9p
  - JAK2V617F (substitute valine to phenylalanine) is the most common; JAK exon 12 mutation
  - Does not require EPO to change to the active conformation
  - Constitutively activated receptor – always phosphorylated and “on”, signalling the downstream pathways

## Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia

- 10-15% of all AML cases; median age = 40 years; 1 M:1 F; increased incidence in Hispanic patients with AML (20-30%)
- Clinical features: B symptoms, symptoms from each cell line affected, tumour lysis, leukostasis
  - High risk of early mortality before onset of therapy or during the early stages of therapy
  - Usually from severe hemorrhage (frequently from fatal cerebral hemorrhage)
- Hypergranular/typical – AML FAB M3; 75% of cases; WBC usually decreased
- Microgranular/hypogranular – AML FAB M3v; 25% of cases; WBC increased > 10
- Confirmatory Dx: karyotype (t(15;17) chromosome), FISH (PML/RAR𝛂 fusion gene), molecular NGS (PML/RARα fusion gene)
- Suggestive Dx: peripheral blood/BM aspiration (myeloblasts with 2+ Auer rods in cytoplasm), flow cytometry (HLA-DR negative)
- Common complications: hemorrhage, abnormal fibrinolysis, DIC, TLS, leukostasis, febrile neutropenia
- Treatment: all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA), arsenic, chemotherapy (high risk)

## Leukemias

- Leukemia = WBC cancer localized to the blood and BM
- Most common cancer in younger patients, and falls out of the top 6 with age
- Etiology: male, older age, smoking, obesity, hematological disease (MDS, PV, ET, CML), chemicals (benzene, alkylating agents, topoisomerase inhibitors), radiation,
hereditary (Down syndrome, Bloom syndrome, Fanconi anemia, ataxia telangiectasia)
- Presentation: blood, skin (leukemia cutis), gums, behind the eyes
- Complications: leukocytosis and infiltration, tumour lysis syndrome (hyperphosphatemia, hyperuricemia, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, acidosis à acute renal failure), DIC
- Myeloid sarcoma = an extramedullary manifestation of acute myeloid leukemia, composed of myeloblasts

## Specialized Testing for Hematological Malignancies

Morphologic assessment of the marrow aspirate – assesses all stages of hematopoiesis
-BM samples should be taken from the posterior superior iliac spine (PSIS)
-Extenuating circumstances: sternum (children and adults), tibia (children only)
-Never take a core biopsy sample from the sternum (too close to the heart), tibia, skull, or ribs
Normal marrow aspirate
Mixed population of erythroid and myeloid cells in various stages of maturation
Ratio of myeloid to erythroid cells is maintained; normal ratio is between 1:1 to 3:1
Morphologically all the cells appear normal (no evidence of dysplasia)
Normal marrow core biopsy
Subcortical acellular zone is often present
Marrow cellularity depends on patients’ age (cellularity = 100 – age)
Cellular composition similar to that seen on aspirate slides
Flow cytometry of the marrow aspirate
-Flow cytometry: a technology used to characterize different cellular populations in a mixture of cells
-Uses the ability of cells to scatter light to measure physical properties of a cell and detect antigens expressed on or in a cell by staining a mixture of cells
with fluorescently labeled antibodies
-Samples: fresh (&lt; 24 hours), liquid (blood, BM aspirates, CSF, ascites fluid), solid if disaggregated and suspended in fluid (lymph node, tissue biopsy)
-Forward scatter (FSC) = size; side scatter (SSC) = cellular complexity/granularity
-Immunophenotype = pattern of antigen expression à markers of immaturity, myeloid markers, B-cell markers, T-cell markers, NK cell markers
Cytogenetics of the marrow aspirate
-Cytogenetics: the study of the chromosomes – determine if there are any changes in chromosome structure, location, or function

Molecular diagnostics of the marrow aspirate
-Molecular genetics: the study of genetic material in chromosomes – determine if there are any mutations in the genetic materials of cells
