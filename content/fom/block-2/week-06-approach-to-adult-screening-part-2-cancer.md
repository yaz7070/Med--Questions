## Neoplasia Terminology

- Mass: an abnormal tissue that takes up space as it grows; benign or malignant
  - Descriptive language: size, shape, surface/borders, colour, consistency
- Tumour/neoplasm: an abnormal mass of tissue which results from excessive cell
division and evasion of apoptosis; benign or malignant
- Dysplasia (pre-malignant): dysfunctional cell growth/maturation and
morphological changes in cell nuclei; only seen under a microscope
- Carcinoma in situ/high grade dysplasia (pre-malignant): dysplastic cells involve
the entire epithelial thickness and push against the intact basement membrane
- Malignant neoplasm: dysplastic cells have broken past the basement membrane
- Metastasis: the process and outcome of cancerous cells spreading and invading
other tissues

## Carcinogenesis

- Cancer development is the result of a multi-step process involving genetic alterations of DNA controlling the hallmarks of cancer
that lead to malignant transformation; rarely progresses far enough to become clinically detectable
- Two-Hit Hypothesis: normal cells = 2 undamaged chromosomes; 1st hit = hereditary OR rare event; 2nd hit = cancer
  - Cancer is rarely caused by one mutation and is usually a cascade of successive mutations that lead to cell instability
- Two stage clonal expansion model of carcinogenesis
  - Initiation – genetic alteration produces premalignant cell from healthy cells
  - Promotion – premalignant cells proliferate
  - Transformation – second rapid alteration produces malignant cell from pool of premalignant cells
  - Progression – from the time of malignant cell to clinical cancer

## Precursor Lesion Pathway

- Many cancers are preceded by precursor lesions, which can sometimes be good targets for preventative and screening efforts
- HPV vaccination (HPV infection) and pap test (cervical pre-cancer) can prevent/screen for squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix

## Cancer Genetic Counselling/Testing

- Consider a referral if a patient has a personal/FHx of cancer, a genetic test is available/can be adequately interpreted, results
may influence medical management, and the patient wants the information
- Review medical/FHx, likelihood of specific cancer syndrome, genes/chromosomes/inheritance, risk of having inherited a familial
cancer gene mutation/developing cancer, cancer surveillance/risk reduction strategies, psychosocial risk Ax
- Benefits: better understand risk of cancer, more accurate cancer risk prediction, relieve anxiety/uncertainty, facilitate cancer
risk management, provide family members with the option of having genetic testing, can lead to finding cancer earlier
- Drawbacks: false positives/negatives, current genetic tests cannot identify all inherited gene changes linked to cancer, there
may not be effective risk reduction/early detection methods, not all family members want to know their risk of developing
cancer, positive test can lead to more worry/medical appointments/tests/procedures
- Best to confirm the presence of a mutation in an affected family member first before testing an unaffected family member
- Avoid testing children for adult-onset conditions – stigmatization, increased anxiety, “vulnerable child” parenting

| Ethical Principle | Application to Genetic Testing |
|---|---|
| Autonomy | It is every patient and family members’ personal choice about whether or not they should have genetic testing, and how to act on the |

results; it is also their right to choose who should know their test results
Beneficence · A key benefit of genetic testing is to identify who in a family is truly at increased risk of cancer, so that they may opt for enhanced
surveillance to promote earlier detection, or prophylactic surgery to significantly reduce their risk
Non-maleficence · Patients who elect to have genetic testing should have genetic counselling to accurately interpret their results and the clinical implications
for them and their family members
Justice · Every individual who is eligible for genetic counselling/testing should be given the option of this service, and based on the outcome, all
options in cancer risk management should be reviewed

## Communicating Bad News – SPIKES

- Setting – privacy, involve significant others, sit down, look attentive and calm, listening mode, availability
- Perception – before you tell, ask; if a patient is in denial, do not confront the denial at the first interview
- Invitation – obtain overt permission to respect the patient’s right to know (or not to know)
- Knowledge – give a warning, avoid medical jargon, use the same language as the patient, give information in small chunks
- Empathy – listen for and identify emotions, identify the cause/source, acknowledge the patient’s emotions as they arise
- Strategy and summary – give the patient an opportunity to voice any major concerns/questions, clear plan of next steps
