## Choosing Wisely

- More is not always better!
- Choosing Wisely encompasses appropriate utilization of tests
- Appropriate utilization is about balancing both underutilization and overutilization of tests
- Inappropriate utilization of tests can cause unnecessary medical procedures and treatment of patients, which can lead to
inappropriate patient care or patient harm
- Inappropriate utilization of tests leads to increased cost burden to the health care system
- Choosing Wisely recommendations are evidence-based and generated by multi-disciplinary stakeholders
- 348 recommendations are intended for clinicians and patients to make informed decisions about the care being provided
- Tool kits are available to help with implementing Choosing Wisely in your practice

| Stakeholder | Why Choosing Wisely? |
|---|---|
| Labs | Revenue, poor quality program, lack of specialty/technical expertise |
| Healthcare systems | Poor quality management systems, inadequate resources, culture/historical practice (silo effect) |
| Physicians | Malpractice fear, patient pressure, incentives, fee for service |
| Patients | More is better, wellness movements, Dr. Google, resource availability, lack of testing knowledge |

## Approach to Unexpected Lab Test Results

1. Understand the issue – not consistent with related tests/previous values for the test/presentation
2. Consider pre-analytical explanations – wrong test, patient preparation, sample collection, transportation, lab processing error
3. Consider analytical explanations – random error, sample mix-up by the lab, methodology difference
4. Consider post-analytical explanations – transcription error, patient mix-up in reporting, inappropriate reference interval
5. Consult with a lab professional – explanation, suggest other investigations/alternate methods for the test
6. Consider additional testing – repeat, different analytical method, Choosing Wisely
7. Consider that the result could be correct

## Homeostatic Mechanisms in Response to Dehydration

1. Restore osmolality – hypothalamus ↑ activity of osmoreceptors, ↓ activity of stretch receptors, releases ADH
2. Conserve fluid – kidneys ↑ permeability and ↓ urine output to ↑ water resorption/prevent further fluid loss
3. Restore fluid volume – hypothalamus ↑ thirst
4. Increase BP

## The Role of RAAS in Sodium Balance

- Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is activated by decreased BP and increased extracellular K+
- Plasma osmolality > regulation of ECF volume
- Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) inhibit RAAS
Angiotensin II à ↑ aldosterone à ↑ Na+ reabsorption à vasoconstriction à ↑ ADH/thirst à ↑ ECF volume, ↑ BP, restore osmolality

## Blood Pressure

- Blood pressure (BP): the pressure exerted by blood on the wall of the blood vessels; arterial pressure in systemic circulation
- R side = pulmonary circulation (CO2 rich, O2 poor blood); L side = systemic circulation (O2 rich, CO2 poor blood)
- Total blood volume = 5 L; heart and pulmonary circulation = 15%, systemic arteries (distribution vessels) = 10%, systemic
capillaries (exchange vessels) = 5%, systemic veins (capacitance vessels) = 70%
- BP = systolic pressure/diastolic pressure = 120 mmHg/80 mmHg
  - Low risk = 120/80, medium risk = 121-139/80-89, high risk = 140+/90
- Physiological factors: age, gender, genetics, diet, sleep, activity, stress, hydration
- Circulatory factors: cardiac output (heart), peripheral resistance (blood vessels)

## Mean Arterial Pressure

- Mean arterial pressure (MAP): the average BP during a single cardiac cycle
MAP = DP + 1/3(SP – DP) = 93 mmHg
MAP = CO × TPR
- Cardiac output (CO): the amount of blood ejected by the heart in one minute (1/min)
CO = HR × SV
- Heart rate (HR): number of contractions in 1 min
- Stroke volume (SV): the amount of blood ejected in one contraction
- Total peripheral resistance (TPR): the total resistance encountered by the blood from all the blood vessels

## Definition of a Mental Disorder

- Mental disorder: a syndrome characterized by clinically significant disturbance in an individual’s cognition, emotion regulation,
or behaviour that reflects a dysfunction in the psychological, biological, or developmental processes underlying mental
functioning – key is the duration of symptoms and the level of interference/distress in social, occupational, or other activities
- An expectable, or culturally approved response to a common stressor or loss is NOT a mental disorder
- Socially deviant behaviour (political, religious, sexual) and conflicts that are primarily between the individual and society are
NOT mental disorders UNLESS the deviance or conflict results from a dysfunction in the individual

## The Inflammatory Response

- Host response to cellular/tissue damage and infection – protective response that is essential for survival
- Processes are co-ordinated by a variety of inflammatory cells, and by the release of cytokines and other mediators
- Inflammation often results in local tissue damage which should resolve upon termination of the inflammatory response
- Harmful consequences: autoimmune disease (misdirected against self-antigens), allergies (exaggerated against normally
harmless environmental agents), anaphylaxis (uncontrolled)
- The Celcus Triad: rubor = redness; calor = heat; dolor = pain; tumor = swelling; functio laesa = loss of function (Virchow)
  - Tumor is often due to protein rich fluid leakage in the tissues, but it may also be due to an abscess (pus)
- Termination: mediators of inflammation are only produced while the stimulus for inflammation persists, have short half-lives,
and are degraded after release; neutrophils die by apoptosis; inflammation triggers termination signals

## General Visceral Afferents and Referred Pain

- Each spinal nerve embryonically provides innervation to a single patch of skin (dermatome) and muscle (myotome), but as the
embryo elongates, the patches converge, mixing nerve supply
- Visceral sensory afferent neuron à thoracoabdominal splanchnic nerve à sympathetic chain à WRC à spinal nerve à DRG
à dorsal root à dorsal grey horn à second order neuron à brain
- Referred pain: brain is used to receiving general sensory information through the spinal cord from general sensory afferents, so
it misinterprets pain from the visceral afferents as body wall pain associated with those spinal levels; diffuse, non-specific, achy
- Pelvic pain line corresponds to the lower limit of the peritoneum
  - Follow sympathetic to T1-L3 spinal levels
  - Follow parasympathetic to S2-4 spinal levels

## Social Determinants of Health and Health Equity

- Life expectancy in full health in Canada: poorest fifth = 50.2 years; richest fifth = 61.4 years
  - Poorest fifth: less likely to use cancer screening services, fewer specialist visits, longer wait times, higher rates of
emergency hospitalizations, avoidable deaths
- Self-rated mental health and COPD hospitalization gaps by income have widened over time
- Cost of health disparity in Canada = \$6.1 billion
- SDH have disproportionately affected marginalized groups during COVID-19
  - Chronic multimorbidities, multigeneration/dense households, work-from-home, self-isolation, rates of testing
- Horizontal equity: equal treatment of equals in relevant respects
- Vertical equity: unequal treatment for those who are unequal in relevant respects

## Critical Appraisal

- Application of rules of evidence to original clinical studies to decide if the studies’ conclusions are valid and useful
- How was the study designed? – consider screening strategies compared and the similarity of the groups at the start of the study
- What are the results? – identify benefits (reduction of disease-specific mortality) and potential harms
  - Lead-time bias: occurs when a disease is detected by a screening test earlier than it would have been if it were
diagnosed by its clinical appearance; time lag of asymptomatic disease is not considered during survival analysis
  - Length bias: an overestimation of survival duration among screened groups due to the disproportionate identification
of asymptomatically slowly progressing cases, while fast progressing cases are detected after giving symptoms
- Will these results help me care for my patients? – consider inclusion/exclusion criteria, result similarity/differences for specific
subgroups, whether the technology of interest has changed since the study was published
