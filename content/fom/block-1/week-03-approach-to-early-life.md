## Goals of “Well Baby” Ax

1. Growth – length, weight, head circumference; correct age until 24 months if &lt; 37 weeks gestation
  - USE WHO GROWTH CHARTS
  - Failure to thrive: weight less than 3% and family norm; weight trajectory crosses 2 major % lines
  - Short stature: height less than 3% and family norm
  - BMI > 85% = overweight, BMI > 95% = obese
2. Parent/caregiver concerns
3. Nutrition – feeding, supplementation, stool pattern/urine output
4. Education and advice – injury prevention, behaviour and family issues, environmental health, other issues
5. Development – inquiry and observation of milestones
6. Physical examination
7. Problems and plans/current and new referrals – medical specialist, dietician, speech, audiology, PT, OT, eyes, dental, social
8. Investigations/screening and immunization

## Red Flags of Abnormal Development

- Persistence of primitive reflexes
- No social smiling or responsive vocalization by 2 months
- Hands still fisted at 4 months
- No response to sound
- Not sitting by 9 months
- Not pointing to objects of interest or desire by 15 months
- Not standing by 15 months
- Not walking by 18 months
- No single words by 18 months
- Excessive drooling and still mouthing at 18 months
- Hand preference at less than 2 years
- Not putting 2 words together by 2 years
- Lack of eye contact and engagement with other children at 2 years
- Not standing briefly on one foot at 3 years
- Speech not intelligible to familiar adults at 3 years
- Unable to hop at 4 years
- Unable to stack 10 blocks and draw a circle at 4 years
- Cannot pick shorter or longer of two lines at 4 years
- Loss of previously acquired skills

## Brain Development

- Pruning: elimination of unused connections and pathways
- Sculpting: enhancement of frequently used connections and pathways
- Critical periods: permanent deficit results if the needed stimuli are not received – visual development
- Sensitive periods: child is primed to receive sensory input; skill acquisition becomes much more difficult if missed – language

## Theories of Development

- Gessell (1928) – wrote detailed descriptions of normal child development
- Erikson (1950) – psychosocial development

- Pavlov & Thorndike – classical conditioning
  - Reinforcement: relationship between a stimulus and response becomes strengthened through repeated association
  - Extinction: relationship between stimulus and response is weakened
  - Generalization: conditioned response becomes linked to a stimulus similar to the initial stimulus
- Skinner – operant conditioning
  - Positive reinforcement: addition of a positive event that increases the behaviour
  - Negative reinforcement: ending of a negative event that increases the behaviour
  - Punishment: addition of an event that decreases the behaviour
  - Response cost: loss of positive reinforcement that results in decreased behaviour/extinction
- Piaget – cognitive development

## Families

- Families: comprised of married or common-law couples, including same-sex couples, living in the same dwelling with or without
children, and lone parents living with at least one child
- Role in raising children: physical needs, emotional support, education and socialization
- No evidence that family structure alone is a significant predictor of child health or developmental outcome
- One person households, numbers of couples without children, and multigenerational families are INCREASING

## Genetic Disorder Consideration

- Developmental delays, dysmorphic features, or congenital malformations
- Positive family history of a genetic disorder
- History of 3+ miscarriages/stillbirths, infertility, neonatal deaths
- Consanguinity/ethnic background – Ashkenazi Jewish (Tay-Sachs, BRCA1, Canavan disease, familial dysautonomia), French
Canadian (Tay-Sachs), Mediterranean (β-thalassemia), Southeast Asian (α-thalassemia), Amish/Mennonite and Arab (various
rare disorders), African (sickle cell anemia), Caucasian (cystic fibrosis)
- Cancers – multiple members, early onset (&lt; 50), multiple primaries in 1 individual, occurrence in less commonly affected sex
- Early onset heart disease/sudden deaths

## Genetics Terminology

- Allelic heterogeneity – different variants in the same gene; different phenotypes
- Locus heterogeneity – mutations in different genes at different chromosomal loci cause the same phenotype
- Penetrance – fraction of individuals who carry a gene who manifest a specific phenotype
  - Complete = absolutely shows signs; incomplete = may not show signs
- Anticipation – symptoms of a disorder become more apparent/severe with each new generation; triple repeat disorders

## Preconception Counselling

- Provision of information and support to a couple planning a pregnancy to promote health and reduce risk
- Primary prevention – review personal and family Hx, and refer if any genetic conditions present
  - Folic acid: 0.8-1mg/d beginning 3 months prior to pregnancy, continue through 1st trimester
  - Avoid smoking, alcohol, drugs, and exposure to saunas/heat stress
- Secondary prevention – discuss options for prenatal screening and diagnosis

termination of pregnancy (before 24 weeks)

## Trisomy 21 Aetiologies

- Nondisjunction during meiosis I or meiosis II (47, XY, +21)
- Robertsonian translocation: extra Chr 21 is attached to Chr 14 (46, der (14;21)(q10;q10), +21)
- Mosaic (46, XY \[20\]/47, XY, +21 \[10\])
- Balanced reciprocal translocation: occurs between non-homologous chromosomes; risk for carrier of stillbirths or miscarriages
and over-/under-expression (46, XY, t(4;20)(q21;q12))

## Chromosome Disorders

- Turner Syndrome – puffy hands and feet at birth, webbed neck, coarctation of aorta, short stature, primary amenorrhea and
infertility, normal intelligence; (45, X; 45, X/46, XX; 46, X, iXq; 45, X/46, XY)
- Trisomy 13: Patau Syndrome – midline defects, cleft palate, polydactyly, severe developmental delay; (47, XX, +13)
- Trisomy 18: Edward Syndrome – cardiac defects, delicate facial features, small size, overlapping fingers, “rocker bottom” feet,
marked developmental delay; (47, XX, +18)
- Klinefelter Syndrome – tall stature, gynecomastia, small testes, learning disability, infertility, normal appearance; (47, XXY)
- DiGeorge Syndrome – cleft palate, congenital heart defect, hypocalcemia, immunodeficiency; (22q11.2 microdeletion)
- Prader-Willi Syndrome – hypotonia, hypogonadism, hypomentia, obesity (15q11-q13 paternal deletion)
- Angelman Syndrome – small head, seizures, happy demeanor, stiff-legged gait with arms up, absent speech, severe
developmental delay; (15q11-q13 maternal deletion)

## Cytogenetic Tests

- Karyotype (G-banding): chromosomes of an individual arranged by pairs according to standard classification
  - Nomenclature: total number of chromosomes, sex chromosomes
  - Group A (1-3), Group B (4-5), Group C (6-12, X), Group D (acrocentric chromosomes: 13-15, 21, 22), Group E (16-18),
Group F (19-20)
  - Shows chromosome structure of genomic imbalance and balanced rearrangements
  - Order for suspected chromosome syndromes, first degree relatives with translocations, 3+ miscarriages, stillbirths or
neonatal death of unknown cause, disorders of sexual differentiation, infertility/amenorrhea
- Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH): uses fluorescent probes that bind only to parts of the chromosome with a high degree
of sequence complementarity
- Microarray: measures the expression levels of large numbers of genes simultaneously
  - Nomenclature: arr(1-22)x2, (XY)x1
  - ACMG: 1 = pathogenic, 2 = likely pathogenic, 3 = variant of uncertain clinical significance, 4 = likely benign, 5 = benign
  - Shows gene content of chromosome imbalance
  - Order for moderate to severe developmental delays and multiple congenital anomalies
- Quantitative fluorescence polymerase chain reaction (QF-PCR): amplifies specific regions of DNA and quantifies the amount of
DNA present in those regions

## Nutrients

- Macronutrients: carbon-based compounds that can be metabolically processed into cellular energy
  - Carbohydrates (4Kcal/gram), protein (4Kcal/gram), lipids (9Kcal/gram) – alcohol (7Kcal/gram)
- Micronutrients: not sources of energy for the body, but play a role as cofactors or coenzymes that facilitate chemical reactions
  - Trace minerals: only required in a few mgs or less – molybdenum, selenium, zinc, iron, iodine
  - Macrominerals: required in hundreds of mgs – calcium, magnesium, potassium, sodium, phosphorus

## Dietary Reference Intakes

- Estimated Average Requirement (EAR): meets the needs of 50% of the population
- Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA): RDA = EAR + 2 SD; meets the needs of 97.5% of the population
- Adequate Intake (AI): used when RDA cannot be calculated
- Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL): highest level of daily nutrient intake that is unlikely to pose risk of adverse health effects to
almost all individuals in the population

## Canada’s Food Guide – Healthy Food Choices

- Eat plenty of vegetables and fruits, whole grain foods, and protein foods; choose proteins that come from plants more often
  - Choose foods with healthy fats instead of saturated fat
- Limit highly processed foods; if you choose these foods, eat them less often and in small amounts
  - Prepare meals and snacks using ingredients that have little to no added sodium, sugars, or saturated fat
  - Choose healthier menu options when eating out
- Make water your drink of choice
  - Replace sugary drinks with water
- Use food labels
- Be aware that food marketing can influence your choices

## Canada’s Food Guide – Canada’s Dietary Guidelines

1. Nutritious foods are the foundation for healthy eating
2. Processed or prepared foods and beverages that contribute to excess sodium, free sugars, or saturated fat undermine healthy
eating and should not be consumed regularly
3. Food skills are needed to navigate the complex food environment and support healthy eating

## Dietary Trends

- Intermittent fasting – cycles between periods of fasting (alternate-day, whole-day, and/or time-restricted feeding), with either
no food or significant calorie reduction, and periods of unrestricted eating
- Gluten-free – eliminates all foods containing or contaminated with gluten
- Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH diet) – fruits, vegetables, low fat milk, whole grains, fish, poultry, beans, nuts
- Ketogenic diet – low-carbohydrate, high-fat
- Mediterranean diet – primarily plant-based; emphasis on healthy fats; fish as the preferred animal protein; water as the main
daily beverage; stresses daily physical activity
- Paleo diet – caveman/Stone-Age diet, includes lean meats, fish, fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds

## Infant Nutrition

- Breastfeeding (exclusive up to 6 months, up to 2 years) + 400 IU/day Vitamin D + iron-containing foods after 6 months
  - 20Kcal/oz, 0.67Kcal/mL – 50% fat, 40% lactose, 10% protein (60-80% whey protein, 20-40% casein)
  - Every 2-3 hours for 10-20 mins/side – high-fat hind milk important
  - Colostrum (IgA, IgM, IgG), transitional milk, mature milk
  - Iron supplementation for high-risk groups: birth weight &lt; 3kg, premature infants &lt; 37 weeks gestation, prenatal alcohol
exposure, maternal diabetes, infants of iron-deficient mothers
- Formula feeding – very important to mix formula correctly to avoid failure to thrive, electrolyte imbalance and/or dehydration
  - Ready to feed, concentration, powder
  - 20Kcal/oz, 0.67Kcal/mL – 50% fat, higher vitamin and mineral content than human milk, iron absorbed less
  - Special formulas: hydrolyzed whey (lower osmolality, improve gastric emptying), soy (vegetarians, lactase deficiency,
galactosemia), lactose free, protein hydrolysate (cannot digest/allergic to intact protein, fat malabsorption)
  - Less stools than breastfed babies

## Canadian Feeding Guidelines for 6-24 Months

- Open cups support development of mature feeding skills from > 6 months
- No honey &lt; 12 months – infantile botulism
- Bottles and sippy cups are discouraged > 12 months
- Homo milk can be given, but cannot replace breastmilk or formula
- Start lumpy
- Iron-containing foods – iron-fortified infant cereals, meat, tofu, legumes, poultry, fish, whole eggs
- No delay in starting allergenic foods – eggs, peanut products
- Parents decide what foods are available and when; children decide whether to eat or not and how much to eat

## Normal Infant Growth

- Newborn: 5-10% weight loss 1-3 DOL, regain birth weight by 10-14 DOL
- Infant: calculate growth velocity (g/day) – double birth weight 4-5 months, triple birth weight 1 year
- Child: 1 year = 10 kg, 5 years = 20 kg, 10 years = 30 kg
- Average weight gain = 2-3 kg/year from 2 years to puberty
- Supine length &lt; 2 years, stadiometer height > 2 years; birth = 50 cm, 4 years = 100 cm
- Average height increase = 5-8 cm/year from 4 years to puberty
- Head circumference: birth = 35 cm; increase 2 cm/month in the first 3 months
