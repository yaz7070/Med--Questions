## Inflammation

- A general term for a wide range of responses from the immune system within a localized tissue; systemic = sepsis
- Swelling, redness, heat, pain, loss of function
- Acute inflammation – tissue repair, healing, and clearance of local pathogens
- Resolution – shifts in cytokines, M1 to M2 macrophages
- Chronic inflammation – tissue remodelling and loss of function over time

## Antibody Function

- Neutralize – block the biological activity of the antigen by binding to its surface
- Opsonize – bind to the surface antigen of the pathogen to facilitate phagocytosis
- Facilitate pathogen recognition and antigenic responses – bind to specialized immune cells
- Activate the complement system – phagocytosis and attracting other immune cells towards the pathogen

Immunization
- Immunologic memory: the immune system’s ability to remember its experience with an infectious agent, leading to effective
and rapid immune response upon subsequent exposure to the same or similar infectious agents
- Passive immunity – transfer of immunoglobulins obtained from human or animal sources, placental transfer
- Active immunity – body produces pathogen-specific antibodies after an antigen is introduced through vaccines

## Ideal Vaccines

- Safe with minimal adverse effects
- Effective in providing lifelong protection against disease after a single dose that can be administered at birth
- Inexpensive
- Stable during shipment and storage
- Easy to administer

## Vaccines

- Individual level – protect the immunized person against the specific disease
- Population level – reduce the incidence of disease; reduce exposure of susceptible individuals and consequent illness
- Vaccine efficacy: the vaccine’s ability to prevent illness in people vaccinated in controlled studied
- Vaccine effectiveness: the vaccine’s ability to prevent illness in people in the real world

## Herd Immunity

- Herd immunity: the immunity of a population against a specific infectious disease
- The resistance of a population to spread an infectious disease is based on the percentage of people who are immune and the
probability that those who are still susceptible will come into contact with an infected person
- The proportion of the population required to be immune to reach herd immunity depends on the transmissibility of the
infectious agent either from a symptomatically infected person or from an asymptomatically colonized person
- High R0 = diseases that are highly infectious and require higher immunization coverage – measles

## School-Age Development (6-12 years)

- In pre-school years, there is semi-logical thinking which leads to misconceptions – “my parents divorced because I was bad”
- School-age children start to differentiate from their parents; peers important, rule-based games, increased attention span
- Learning disabilities, behaviour disorders, and intellectual disability may become evident during the school year
- Increasing difficulty may occur as the educational demands increase
- Mild deficits in learning may not present until school age
- Diagnosing specific learning disabilities requires that the child have “significant” academic deficit – 2 grades below expectations

## Factors Affecting School Readiness

- Physical health and fitness – hearing, vision, chronic conditions (surgical repairs of congenital disorders, medications)
- Attitude and motivation – understand school is important and will be hard, able to delay gratification to some degree
- Attentional readiness – sit and attend, know when to interrupt, listen without eye contact, assimilate verbal information
- Emotional readiness – tolerate separation from parent, allow correction, make friends
- Cognitive readiness – achievement of previous milestones
- Cultural readiness – family feels school is important, school and family are congruent
- Academic readiness – reading (rhyming), writing (fine motor dexterity), arithmetic (symbolic thinking, number concepts)
- If a child is not going to be ready, try learning in smaller groups (preschool), remediation of identified deficits, delaying school
entry, and/or specialized programming

## General Approach to Childhood Behaviours

- Take a detailed history including the ABCs (antecedent, behaviour, consequence)
- Ax development – make use of assessment tools
- PEx – consider underlying physical conditions
- Management plan – typical behaviour = anticipatory guidance; atypical behaviour = further Ax and specific Tx

## Common Childhood Clinical Presentations

- Hidden causes of discomfort in young children: headache, undiagnosed seizures, entropion, otitis media, sinusitis, dental caries,
mouth ulcers, pharyngitis, medication for asthma, GERD, constipation, UTI, renal stones, arthralgia/arthritis, occult fracture, hair
tourniquet, eczema/dermatitis, skin breakdown/ulcer
- Colic (5-20%) – increased frequency/intensity of crying in young infants who do not have underlying illness
  - Prolonged crying (> 3 hours/day), persistent (> 3 days/week for > 3 weeks), especially evenings, peaks at 6 weeks
  - Tx: careful evaluation of causes for discomfort, reassurance, support/respite
- Night waking (25%) – sleep patterns are highly variable in infancy; cultural expectations have a significant influence
  - Tx: sleep hygiene, appropriate limit setting, eliminate bottle at 12 months, reassurance/nurturing
- Headbanging (under 2 years) – typically when a toddler is attention seeking, avoiding a request, or self-soothing
  - Management: careful Hx and PEx, encourage “time-in”, redirection/distraction
- Infant masturbation (most children) – may be mistaken for seizures/movement disorder; quickly learn to keep “private”
  - Management: careful evaluation, caregiver education, encourage “time-in”, redirection/distraction
- Temper tantrums (18-36 months) – out of control behaviour (screaming, stomping, hitting, headbanging, falling down,
frustration/distress)
  - Part of normal assertion of independence and “testing” behaviour, brief (&lt; 5 mins), improves with age, breath-holding
  - Management: careful evaluation, parent behaviour management strategies, consider DDx (sensory impairments,
communication disorders, seizures, abuse/neglect, family violence, inappropriate expectations, neurological
conditions, emerging mental health disorders)
- Aggression/anger – requires a multimodal approach

## ADHD

- Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD): a neurodevelopmental disorder defined by a persistent pattern of inattention
and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development
- Causes: highly heritable, gene-environment interaction, neurochemical/structural/functional brain abnormalities
- Tx: psychoeducation, parent training/behavioural management, school accommodation, stimulants, non-stimulants
Diagnostic Criteria – thorough Hx (parent interview, child interview, consider multiple settings), rating scales (SNAP-IV), psychological tests
A. 6+ inattentive symptoms and/or 6+ hyperactive-impulsive symptoms that have persisted for at least 6 months, inconsistent with developmental level, with
direct negative impact on social and academic/occupational activities
B. Several symptoms were present prior to age 12 years
C. Several symptoms are present in two or more settings – home, school, work, with friends/relatives, other activities
D. There is clear evidence that the symptoms interfere with, or reduce the quality of, social, academic, or occupational functioning
E. · The symptoms do not occur exclusively during the course of schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder and are not better explained by another mental
disorder – mood disorder, anxiety disorder, dissociative disorder, personality disorder, substance intoxication, withdrawal

## Health Canada Social Determinants of Health and Health Inequalities

- Income and social status – low income, income inequality, social exclusion
- Employment and working conditions – safety at work, stress at work, social environment
- Education and literacy – job opportunities, social-psychological status, healthy lifestyle
- Childhood experiences – toxic stress alters brain development, negative coping skills, ACEs effects across the lifespan
  - Latency: exposure at one point in the life course will have an effect on health years to decades later, despite any
intervening experiences
  - Accumulation: multiple exposures over the life course will have individual health effects, but will also combine to
create more significant effects
  - Pathways: a sequence of exposure in which one stage of life influences the probability of the exposures occurring later
in life; the early exposure sets off a pathway towards experiencing further adversity
- Physical environments – walkability, housing, neighbourhood density
- Social supports and coping skills – system supports, personal coping style, adverse life events
- Healthy behaviours – smoking and substance abuse, diet
- Access to health services – proximity, privileges, competing pressures
- Biology and genetic endowment – epigenetics, interaction of genes and environment, genetic predisposition
- Gender – discrimination, gender pay gap, childcare responsibilities
- Culture – religious beliefs, cultural views on illness and health, cultural healing practices
- Race and racism – vulnerable populations, marginalization, discrimination

## H.E.A.D.S.S. Psychosocial Interview for Adolescents

- Home and environment
- Education and employment
- Activities
- Drugs
- Sexuality
- Suicide/depression

## The Developmental Approach

- Explain confidentiality and capacity to make treatment decisions
- Interview without parents – encourage collaboration with parents when possible/safe
- Ask open-ended questions, avoid medical jargon, be genuine/curious
- Elicit/check the youth’s understanding of their health concerns when providing health education
- Be mindful of self-image, treatment adherence, transition care (pediatric to adult), and outcomes

## The Informing Interview for a Child with Disability

- Health care provider is familiar to the parents
- Both parents are present
- Diagnosis is given in a timely manner
- Time is available at subsequent meetings to review information again

## Giving the News of a Disability

- Introduce self
- Sit down if parents are agreeable
- Warning phrase
- Get to the point quickly
- Allow parents time to absorb the information
- Be comfortable with silence/remain present
