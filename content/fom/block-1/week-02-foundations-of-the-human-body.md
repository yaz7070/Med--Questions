## Cartilage Organization

- Fibrous layer of perichondrium – fibroblasts secrete type II collagen
- Chondrogenic layer of perichondrium – chondroblasts secrete cartilage matrix
- Appositional growth – chondrocytes divide and add matrix
- Interstitial growth – chondrocytes divide within the cartilage forming isogeneic groups

## Types of Bone

- Compact/lamellar bone – osteons (Haversian systems) carry blood vessels with concentric bone lamellae; Volkmann’s canals
connect osteons; Sharpey’s fibers project into the outer circumferential lamellar system
- Spongy/cancellous/trabecular bone – forms a network of anastomosing trabeculae that form interconnecting spaces containing
bone marrow

## Bone Cells

- Osteoprogenitor – bone marrow derived mesenchymal stem cell; in periosteum and endosteum; differentiates into osteoblasts
- Osteoblast – actively secretes bone matrix (osteoid); in osteogenic layer of periosteum and endosteum
- Osteocyte – originates from osteoblasts; forms interconnected cellular network in bone matrix; contacts each other through
canaliculi; nutrient transport; mechanosensory and regulates osteoblasts/osteoclasts
- Osteoclast – originates from fusion of peripheral blood monocytes/macrophages; in Howship’s lacunae; multinucleated;
resorbs bone; important in bone remodelling and calcium/phosphate homeostasis

## Bone Remodelling

1. Resting stage – osteoclast recruitment and activation
2. Bone resorption – osteoblast recruitment and activation, osteoclast removal
3. Transition – matrix synthesis
4. Bone formation
- Intramembranous = without cartilage template – skull, maxilla, mandible, pelvis, clavicle, subperiosteal surface of long bones
- Endochondral = replaces cartilage template – length of long bones, vertebrae, pelvis, base of skull

## The Sarcomere and the Sliding Filament Model

- I band = isotropic; divided by a Z line (disc)
- A band = anisotropic; divided by an H zone and M line
- Sarcomere = Z line to Z line; functional unit of muscle
- I bands and H zones shorten during muscle contraction, sarcomeres shorten
- Troponin complex = T-troponin (tropomyosin binding activity), I-troponin (inhibitory activity – binds actin), C-troponin (calcium
binding activity)
- Tropomyosin = binds actin and blocks myosin binding in the absence of Ca2+

## Neurons

- Motor neurons (efferent) – multipolar neurons convey impulses from brain, spinal cord, and ganglia to effector cells
- Sensory neurons (afferent) – pseudo-unipolar or bipolar neurons in ganglia convey impulses from receptors to the CNS
- Integrative (interneurons) – multipolar neurons form integrating networks between sensory and motor neurons
- Axonal transport on microtubules needed to deliver vesicles to synapse
- Neurons do NOT divide, but can have peripheral nerve repair; sprouts generated, Schwann cells re-innervate the muscle

## Myelination

- Schwann cells (PNS) – one cell myelinates only a segment of a single axon
- Oligodendrocytes (CNS) – one cell myelinates many axons
- Myelin sheath consists of concentric layers of lipid membranes and proteins that insulate the axon

## Nervous System Supporting Cells

- Astrocytes – form a scaffolded network in the CNS between capillaries, fibers, and neurons; maintain blood-brain barrier
- Microglia – transform into phagocytic cells in damaged CNS; defence system; migratory
- Ependymal cells – form the simple epithelial lining of the ventricles and spinal canal; ciliated for propulsion of CSF

## Homeostatic Control Systems

- Sensors – detect changes and transmit an input signal
- Integrating centers – process the information
- Effectors – receive and process the output signal

## Body Fluid Compartments

TBW = total body water (60% of body weight)
ICF = intercellular fluid (K+)
ECF = extracellular fluid (Na+, Cl-)

- Hypo/hypernatremia = Na+ → Addison’s disease, diabetes insipidus
- Hypo/hyperkalemia = K+ → cardiac arrest
- Hypo/hypercalcemia = Ca2+

## Darrow-Yannet Diagrams

- Help determine the net movement of water across the cell membrane and look at relative changes in volume and osmolality
- Changes always occur to the ECF compartment first

## Cellular Transport

- Simple diffusion – passive transport, concentration gradient; small lipophilic molecules (O2, CO2, steroid hormones, alcohol)
- Facilitated diffusion – carrier proteins, concentration gradient; larger polar molecules
- Active transport – carrier-mediated transport, against concentration gradient, requires ATP; Na+/K+ pump, Ca2+ pump
  - Primary – energy is derived directly from the breakdown of ATP
  - Secondary – uses the potential energy from the concentration gradient generated by a primary transport
    - Symport = same direction; antiport = opposite directions
- Osmosis – movement of water down its concentration gradient
  - Osmolality = \# of Osmol/kg of water
  - Osmolarity = \# of Osmol/L of solution; ECF osmolarity = 290mOsM
  - Tonicity: the ability of a solution to cause osmosis across a biological membrane
- Factors that affect the rate of diffusion through cell membranes: concentration gradient, lipid solubility, molecular size,
membrane area, composition of lipid layer

## Cell Signalling

- Autocrine signalling – cell signals to itself
- Paracrine signalling – release of chemical messengers over relatively short distances
- Cell-cell contact signalling – intracellular mediators diffuse between gap junctions
- Synaptic signalling – nerve cells transmit signals via release of neurotransmitters
- Endocrine signalling – hormones are produced by specialized cells and released into the bloodstream, and travel to target cells

## Resting Membrane Potential

- RMP = -70 mV
- Highly permeable to K+ and slightly permeable to Na+ and Cl-
- K+ and Cl- are the most important ions involved in the generation of the RMP
- Leak potassium channels contribute to the establishment of a negative RMP
- Na+/K+ pump maintains the concentration gradient for Na+ and K+ by moving 3 Na+ OUT and 2 K+ IN

## The Action Potential

- All-or-none response and unidirectional
- Depolarization: movement of the membrane potential towards zero
- Threshold: voltage at which an action potential is triggered
- Repolarization: return of the membrane potential to its resting value
- Hyperpolarization: movement of the membrane potential to more
negative values
- Absolute refractory period: no stimulus will excite the nerve; inactivated
V.G. Na+ channels
- Relative refractory period: stronger than normal stimulus is required;
some V.G. Na+ channels have recovered
1. Stimulate nerve
2. Membrane depolarizes (-70 mV to -55 mV)
3. V.G. Na+ channels open very rapidly
4. Na+ flows into the cell
5. Action potential is initiated
6. Membrane depolarizes rapidly to +45 mV
7. V.G. K+ channels begin opening while V.G. Na+ channels become inactivated
8. Eventually all V.G. K+ channels are open and all V.G. Na+ channels are inactivated
9. K+ rushes out of the cell
10. Membrane repolarizes (+45 mV to -70 mV)
11. K+ continues to leave the cell as V.G. K+ channels close slowly
12. Membrane hyperpolarizes to -90 mV
13. V.G. K+ channels close
14. Membrane potential returns to normal (-70 mV) and V.G. channels return to resting configuration

## Neuromuscular Junction

- Motor neuro pre-synaptic axon terminal – filled with synaptic vesicles
- Synaptic cleft – space between the pre- and post-synaptic cells
- Post-synaptic membrane of skeletal muscle fiber
1. AP on motor nerve depolarizes axon terminal and V.G. Ca2+ channels open
2. Ca2+ flows in and Ach is released – larger \[Ca2+\] = more Ach
3. Ach diffuses across the synaptic cleft to the end plate
4. Ach binds to nicotinic receptors and chemically gated channels open
5. Na+ flows into the muscle cell and K+ flows out – local depolarization, so no AP yet
6. Depolarization causes an end plate potential (EPP)
7. EPP triggers V.G Na+ channels to open, triggering an AP

## Excitation contraction coupling (ECC)

8. AP generated at the end plate of the muscle cell propagates over the sarcolemma and down the T-tubules
9. Voltage sensors in the T-tubule detect the AP and change shape
10. Voltage sensor opens Ca2+ channels on the lateral sac of the sarcoplasmic reticulum
11. Ca2+ binds to troponin, causing tropomyosin to roll off the myosin binding sites found on actin
12. Myosin attaches to actin (crossbridge formation) and power stroke occurs
13. Actin (thin myofilaments) slides over myosin (thick myofilaments) and muscle contracts (sliding filaments)
- The muscle will continue contracting as long as there are stimuli at the NMJ, Ach is available, and Ca2+ are in the sarcoplasm
- Relaxation – Ach is broken down by acetylcholinesterase and taken back up into the axon terminal; Ca2+ is actively pumped back
into the sarcoplasmic reticulum by Ca2+ ATPase; tropomyosin covers myosin binding sites and muscle relaxes
- Thin myofilaments – G-Actin (myosin binding sites), tropomyosin (covers myosin binding sites), troponin (regulatory protein)
- Thick myofilaments – many individual myosin molecules (heads contain actin binding sites and ATP binding sites)

## Electrodiagnostic Testing

- Myopathy: nerve conduction can be normal, but compound muscle amplitude is reduced
- Neuropathy: nerve conduction is expected to be abnormal, especially in demyelination diseases
- Nerve conduction studies – evaluates the speed and strength of conduction of an AP by the nerve fiber
- Electromyography – measures the direct electrical activity of the muscle; evaluates insertional and resting activity of muscles
- Repetitive nerve stimulation – nerve fiber is stimulated in a repetitive fashion and the response is recorded from the muscles

## Imaging

- Radiograph – radiation x-rays; need a minimum of 2 radiographs at 90° (anteroposterior, posteroanterior, lateral, oblique)
  - Silhouetting: anatomical structures become distinguishable on radiographs due to different tissue opacities that
surround the structure
  - Summation/superimposition: overlapping of structures; may mislead the reader into thinking there is an abnormality
  - Cannot differentiate muscles and tendons; cannot visualize the brain and internal joint structures
- CT – radiation x-rays; thin 3D slices (axial)
  - Voxel: small 2D measurement of volume
  - Accuracy and precision increases the likelihood that small lesions and abnormalities will be detected
  - Can visualize the brain and heart; cannot visualize internal joint structures
  - More expensive, much larger dose of ionizing radiation
  - Not recommended for pregnant women
- MRI – powerful magnet and radio waves
  - Echo time (TE): measurement of the energy signal received at a specific time after the radiofrequency coil pulse
  - Repetition time (TR): measurement of the signals received as the pulse is turned on and off
  - T1-weighted image: TE and TR are short – show fat best, but are fat-suppressed; mostly grey/black image
  - T2-weighted: TE and TR are long – show damaged or pathologic tissue (fluids) best; bright signal
  - Proton density weighted image: TE is short and TR is long – reveal dense tissue well (cartilage, tendon and joint pain)
  - Most expensive, longest time to produce an image
  - Contraindications: cardiac pacemakers, cochlear implants, ferro-magnetic metal implants
- Ultrasound/sonography – high frequency sound waves
  - Diverse, safe, lowest cost – increasingly being used in diagnostic and therapeutic medicine

- Medical reversal: when a new clinical trial, superior to predecessors by virtue of better controls, design, size, or endpoints,
contradicts current clinical practice; undermines trust in the medical system
- Critical appraisal: process of carefully and systematically assessing the outcome of scientific research to judge its
trustworthiness, value, and relevance in a particular context
  - PICO framework: patient/problem/population, intervention, comparison/control/comparator, outcomes
- Meta-analysis: combines individual effects of a number of studies addressing the same question to produce a summary effect
- Randomized controlled trials: allocation of subjects to experimental or control group by chance
  - Simple randomization, block randomization, stratified randomization
- Prospective cohort study: exposure and non-exposure are confirmed as they occur during the study
- Retrospective cohort study: exposure is confirmed from past records
- Case-control study: cases are compared with controls with respect to exposure
  - Incident cases = newly diagnosed; can identify risk factors, but must wait a period of time and excludes deaths
  - Prevalent cases = diagnosed for a period of time; can study right away, but may identify characteristics of survival

- Cross-sectional study: exposure and outcome are measured at the same point in time
- Case report/series: detailed description of one or a few cases without a control group
- Blinding – open label (all parties aware), single blind (patient/clinician unaware), double blind (patient + investigator unaware),
triple blind (patient + investigator + analysts unaware), unblinding (disclosure of allocation)
- Confounder: an extraneous variable whose presence affects the variables being studied so that the results do not reflect the
actual relationship between the variables under study
- Lead time: the interval between the diagnosis made by a screening program (preclinical phase) and the diagnosis made by the
presentation of signs and symptoms (clinical phase)
- Intention-to-treat: analysis of study groups according to the treatment they were assigned, NOT what they received, if any
- Per-protocol analysis: only includes patients who completed the treatment they were originally allocated
- Number needed to treat (NNT): an estimate of the number of individuals that would need to be treated with the new therapy

- Type I error (α): study sample shows a difference between treatment groups when no difference exists
- Type II error (β): study sample shows no difference between treatment groups when a difference exists

- Two-sided test: investigators are unsure of the direction of the difference in response rate between the two groups
- Internal validity – correct study methodology
- External validity – representativity of the reference population, generalizability
- Reliability – intrasubject variation, intraobserver variation, interobserver variation
- Sensitivity: the ability of a test to identify people with disease; positive disease/positive no disease
- Specificity: the ability of a test to identify people without a disease; negative no disease/no disease
